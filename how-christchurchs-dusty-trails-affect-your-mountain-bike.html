How Christchurch's Dusty Trails Affect Your Mountain Bike

Christchurch is a fantastic place to ride mountain bikes, but our local riding conditions can be hard on your equipment. I'm writing this in November, and yet the weekend's ride left a good coating of dust all over my bike.

While a bit of dirt and dust is unavoidable when riding off-road, understanding how it affects your bike and knowing how to minimise its impact can save you money in the long run.

Here are some of the components that are particularly affected by Christchurch's riding conditions, and what you can do to keep your bike running smoothly.

Your drivetrain takes the biggest hit

One of the first places you'll notice the effects of dusty riding is your drivetrain. Your chain, cassette and chainring are constantly moving and rely on chain oil/ lubrication to keep them moving smoothly.

When riding in dusty conditions these dust particles stick to the chain lubricant, effectively creating an abrasive paste.

Over time, this accelerates wear on your chain and the teeth of your cassette and chainring. It can also lead to increased friction, making your drivetrain noisier and less efficient.

One of the best things you can do to prevent this is to regularly clean your chain and reapply an appropriate lubricant. It's worth remembering that more lubricant isn't necessarily better. Applying excessive amounts can actually encourage dirt and dust to accumulate on your chain.

During the dusty months, I'd recommend using a chain lubricant labelled "dry". While the name refers to its suitability for dry conditions, it also describes how the lubricant behaves once applied. Many dry lubricants use a carrier that evaporates, leaving behind a lubricating film. This tends to attract less dust and dirt than traditional wet lubricants.

If you find your chain and cassette is black with dust and grime, one of my services along with a drivetrain clean may be a good idea. I’ll remove the chain, cassette and chainring from the bike, clean them thoroughly and then reinstall them.

Your suspension doesn't escape either

Mountain bike suspension is designed to operate in some pretty harsh environments, but that doesn't mean it's immune to the effects of dirt and dust.

Your fork and rear shock rely on seals to keep dirt and moisture out while retaining the lubrication required for smooth operation. As the suspension compresses and extends, the wiper seals continually pass over the stanchions, collecting dust along the way. This dust will accumulate around the seals and over time can make its way into places it shouldn’t be!

Wiping down your stanchions after riding is a simple way to minimise the amount of dirt accumulating around the seals. Avoid using high-pressure water around suspension seals, as this can force contaminants further into areas they're supposed to stay out of.

It's also important to keep up with suspension servicing. The oil and grease inside your fork and shock doesn't last forever, and dirty and degraded oil can affect both performance and component longevity. Most manufacturers recommend suspension servicing every 50 hours of riding. For more info on this, take a look at my article on suspension servicing.

Bearings and suspension pivots

Bikes are made up of numerous moving parts, and many of these rely on bearings to operate smoothly. Your headset, bottom bracket, wheel hubs and suspension pivots all use bearings to allow components to rotate Most modern bicycle bearings are sealed, but these seals aren't completely impervious to the environment.

Over time, dirt and moisture can work their way past the seals, contaminating the grease inside the bearing. Once this happens, the bearing can begin to wear prematurely.

In a full service I clean out old, dusty grease and reassemble these parts with fresh grease. This extends the life of the bearings and also keeps and creaking at bay. If necessary, worn bearings can be replaced during this service too.

What can you do to protect your bike?

Fortunately, there are a few relatively simple things you can do to reduce the effects of dusty riding.

Clean your drivetrain regularly. Don't wait until your chain is completely black and covered in grime. A clean chain will last longer and work better. If done regularly, I find that scrubbing your chain and cassette down with a dish brush and the garden hose is quick and easy while delivering decent results. Once it's dry, apply fresh chain lube in advance of your next ride so that it can set before you head out.

Wipe down your suspension stanchions and seals. A quick wipe after each ride helps remove dust and dirt before it has a chance to accumulate excessively around your seals.

Don't neglect servicing. Even if your bike looks relatively clean and is working, components such as suspension and bearings can accumulate dust and grime internally. One of my full services as well as a fork and shock service will help these parts last much longer, saving money in the long run while keeping your bike smooth and reliable.
